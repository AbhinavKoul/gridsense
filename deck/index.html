GridSense

Forecast where Ireland's load will peak. Route renewables there first. From Kerry to Belfast.

The problem

Ireland's wind is there. It doesn't reach the loads that need it.

37.6%of island electricity was renewable, Oct 2025 – Oct 2026 (EirGrid, 15-min actuals, ROI + NI)
23.2%of ROI metered electricity goes to data centres, as flat 24/7 baseload (CSO MEC02, 2025)
  • On windy nights, wind farms are dispatched down because demand isn't there to absorb them.
  • At the evening peak, gas fills the gap and the biggest loads run on it.
  • Nobody can see where renewable power actually goes, by county, by hour.
Ireland's data-centre power fight · 2022 – 2026

The grid said no. The rules changed.

  • Jan 2022 EirGrid: no new data-centre connections in Dublin until 2028.
  • Apr 2024 AWS limits capacity in its Dublin region. GPU nodes "maxed out power-wise"; customers pointed to Sweden and other EU regions.
  • Aug 2024 Google's Grange Castle expansion refused over grid capacity (overturned on appeal, Sep 2026).
  • Dec 2025 CRU's new policy: new data centres must bring their own dispatchable generation or storage, and source 80% of annual demand from new Irish renewables within 6 years.
  • Apr 2026 Amazon backs a €150m Midlands wind farm to power its data centres.

Where GridSense fits

  • Operators must now prove how renewable their load is. GridSense measures it hour by hour.
  • It forecasts where the next peak will hit and books recovered wind for it, easing the peak that triggered the moratorium.
  • Councils and CRU get the same per-area numbers.

The moat: others report national averages after the fact. GridSense forecasts where and when each region will peak, at 2.8% error, and routes renewables there before it happens.

Electrons can't be steered. "Routing" means scheduling transmission, storage and renewable allocation against the forecast, so wind reaches the peak instead of being constrained off.

Next steps

Two bets beyond the prototype.

1 · Green-hour tokens

Rate-limit the island's forecast renewable output into hourly tokens, the same way an API rations requests.

  • Companies earn priority access to green hours by funding new renewables.
  • A per-company cap stops any one buyer from cornering the wind.
  • Unused tokens go back to the pool for everyone else.
  • Every token spent is an auditable hourly record for CSR and Scope 2 reports.

2 · Micro-turbines on Ireland's canals

The Grand, Royal, Barrow and Shannon–Erne waterways and their feeders were built to manage water flow. That flow can turn small turbines too.

  • Fit small turbines at locks, weirs and sluices, where the water drops.
  • Unlike wind, the output is steady day and night.
  • GridSense picks sites where local generation is lowest compared with demand.
  • Partners: Waterways Ireland, local councils and community energy groups.

Canal water moves slowly, so most of the energy is at the drops (locks and weirs), not in the open channel. A pilot at one lock would show the real yield.
