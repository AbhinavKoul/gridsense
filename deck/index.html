EirGrid Smart Grid Dashboard, ROI hourly, 29–30 Sep 2026. Switched-off wind estimated as wind forecast − actual when the grid is ≥ 60% renewable.

  • Two real days from EirGrid. Overnight and midday, the grid is over 60% renewable and wind farms are dispatched down.
  • At 18:00–20:00 demand peaks, there is no surplus left, and gas fills the gap.
  • About 10% of Irish wind is dispatched down every year. EirGrid only publishes island-wide numbers, so nobody sees where the power goes, by county, by hour.
The problem

Data centres now use 23.2% of the Republic's electricity.

6.2×more than in 2015

Technical details · the model

Step 2: share the national load out to 45 areas.

tile size = electricity really used+ NI: 11 districts, by population✓ adds up to EirGrid demand, ±1%
  • 23.2% of ROI metered electricity is data centres (CSO MEC02, last 4 quarters). Modelled as flat baseload at the clusters, with small shares in Kildare, Cork and Clare.
  • The rest is split by real 2025 CSO MEC03 consumption. Dublin postal districts mapped to the four Dublin councils; city/county pairs (Cork, Galway, Limerick, Waterford, Tipperary) split by population.
  • MEC03 'Not coded' (~30%, mostly large users) is left to the data-centre term.
  • Limit: each area is assumed to follow the national hourly shape.
Technical details · forecasting

Step 3: forecast every hour of next week.

how: average of the same hour over the last 8 weeks × last week's trend
  • Method: for each grid take 8 weeks of hourly demand, average by hour of the week (every Tuesday 18:00…), multiply by a trend factor = last week's actual ÷ what the averages predicted.
  • Backtest: forecast last week as if it hadn't happened yet, compare with EirGrid actuals. MAPE 2.82% ROI, 5.47% NI.
  • Area forecast = its data-centre share + its MEC03 share of the regional forecast.
  • Why simple: demand is driven by time of week; this is transparent and hard to overfit. Upgrade: weather inputs, gradient boosting.
Technical details · forecasting

Step 4: rank where next week's peaks hit.

The biggest peaks get first call on spare wind →

  • Each area's highest forecast hour over the next 7 days, from the 168-hour forecast.
  • Routing also uses each day's 6 highest forecast hours.
  • Corridor combined peak (Dublin City, Fingal, South Dublin, Meath): 1,845 MW.
Technical details · routing

Step 5a: find the wind that gets switched off.

  • Dispatch-down estimate = EirGrid wind forecast − actual wind, counted only when the grid is ≥ 60% renewable. Gives 9.2% of ROI wind, 12.2% of NI; EirGrid reports ~10%.
  • Assumption: 50% is recoverable (the network-constraint share, per EirGrid annual reports) by scheduling transmission and storage against the forecast.
  • The pool each day = 50% of that day's dispatch-down.
Technical details · routing

Step 5b: share it out, biggest peaks first.

into each day's 6 highest forecast hourscapped at the area's gas-fired load in those hoursleftover re-shared
  • Allocation weight = forecast peak². Biggest peaks get first call, but not winner-takes-all.
  • Each area is capped at its fossil load in the peak hours, so it never goes past 100% renewable; overflow is re-shared among areas still under their cap (water-filling).
  • Numbers: week-ahead plan, 12.8 GWh in total. Dublin City 3.5 GWh, Fingal 1.9, South Dublin 1.2, Cork County 0.8, Meath 0.5.
Technical details · routing

Step 5c: every flow has a source and a number.

thicker = more energy, next 7 dayseach wind area supplies in line with its wind capacity12.8 GWh planned
  • Dispatch-down happens at wind farms, so each area supplies the pool in proportion to its wind capacity.
  • Shown: the 6 biggest suppliers. Cork County 1.3 GWh, Kerry 1.3, Donegal 1.0, Galway County 0.94, Derry & Strabane 0.72.
  • CO₂ saved is priced at the real carbon intensity of each peak hour the wind displaces.
  • 'Routing' = scheduling transmission, storage and renewable allocation against the forecast, not steering electrons. Starts once 2 weeks of history exist.
