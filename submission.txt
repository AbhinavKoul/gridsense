GridSense: forecast where Ireland's load will peak, and route renewables there first.

Ireland's grid has a timing problem. Data centres now use 23.2% of the Republic's electricity (CSO), 6.2× more than in 2015. That led to Dublin's 2022 grid moratorium and pushed AWS to build elsewhere. Meanwhile about 10% of Irish wind is dispatched down because the power can't reach the demand at the right moment. EirGrid only publishes grid-wide numbers, so nobody can see where and when the next peak will hit.

GridSense can. It forecasts hourly load for every one of the island's 45 areas, seven days ahead, with 2.8% error on a real backtest. It ranks the coming peaks (this week: Dublin City 696 MW on Tuesday at 18:00, then Fingal and South Dublin). Then it books wind that would otherwise be curtailed for those peaks first: Cork, Kerry, Donegal and Galway wind flows to Dublin, scheduled through transmission and storage before the peak arrives. Customers don't have to change their usage.

What sets it apart:
• Predictive, not reactive. Most energy dashboards show you what already happened. GridSense plans a week ahead and books renewables for a peak before it arrives.
• Area by area, and all-island. All 45 areas: 34 ROI local authorities and 11 NI districts. Grid data is national; GridSense turns it into a local plan.
• Every flow has a number. Each line on the map shows the energy it carries, for example Cork County → Dublin City, 485 MWh this week. Every area shows its CO₂ cut, for example Fingal −9.3 kt over 12 months.
• Real data and a working product. 12 months of EirGrid 15-minute data covering ROI and NI, plus CSO metered consumption by county and Dublin postal district. Every number is labelled as measured or modelled.
• Modelled impact on 12 months of real data: wind + solar share rises from 37.6% to 39.1%, putting 621 GWh of wind to work and avoiding 71.2 kt of CO₂ a year. Load stays the same.
• Hourly-matched proof that data centres can use for 24/7 carbon-free and Scope 2 reporting. EirGrid, the CRU and government can use the same evidence for connection and siting decisions.

What's next:
1. Green-hour tokens. Companies reserve priority access to renewable hours with tokens. That turns corporate social responsibility into a measurable, tradeable commitment, and funds new renewables.
2. Canal micro-turbines. Ireland has hundreds of kilometres of canals and water-management channels. Small in-flow turbines can add steady local renewable supply. GridSense maps where it would cover the biggest peaks.
3. Live routing with EirGrid and SONI. Replace our dispatch-down estimate with their real constraint data and generator lists, then route against live forecasts.
4. Per-Eircode precision. ESB Networks smart-meter data takes the forecast from county level to street level, so any household or data centre can plug in its own meter.
5. Siting intelligence. Tell new data centres and factories where wind is in surplus (Kerry, Donegal, Leitrim), before they apply to connect.
